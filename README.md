# Adaptive Policy Design for Community Self-Governance

**An interactive research prototype for adaptive policy design under heterogeneous starting conditions and incomplete evidence.**

Case application: Territorial Public Self-Government (TOS), Voronezh Region, Russia.

> **Public prototype disclaimer**
>
> This is an independent academic policy-design prototype developed for educational and research purposes. It is not an adopted regional policy, an evaluation of individual community organizations, or a representation of confidential project data. The diagnostic profiles and policy pathways shown here are illustrative analytical configurations rather than classifications of actual TOS organizations.

## What this project is

This repository contains the public version of a larger academic policy-design model. It explores a recurring decision problem: how to choose proportionate interventions when community self-governance arrangements or bodies differ in their starting conditions, similar visible symptoms may have different causes, and available evidence is incomplete.

The public prototype focuses on transferable decision logic rather than exposing the full academic model.

## The policy-design problem

The same visible pattern can arise for different reasons. For example, a small active core may reflect exclusion from decisions, but it may also reflect voluntary delegation, specialization, task cycles, or the absence of demand for additional roles.

The core proposition is therefore:

> **Similar symptoms should not automatically trigger identical policy responses.**

## Core framework

The public interface presents the following sequence:

**Diagnosis → Competing explanations → Safeguard check → Conditional pathway → Resource fit → Checkpoint → Continue / Modify / Switch / Pause / Maintain / Stop**

The framework explicitly treats uncertainty as part of the decision problem:

> **Unknown is not zero.**

## What the prototype demonstrates

- diagnostic configurations rather than a single organizational score;
- competing explanations for similar visible patterns;
- proportionate and potentially reversible pilot logic;
- non-compensatory safeguards;
- adaptive checkpoints and pathway revision;
- legitimate “maintain” and “no additional intervention” outcomes;
- explicit limits on generalization and scaling.

## What it does not claim

The prototype does **not**:

- classify actual TOS organizations in the Voronezh Region;
- estimate the prevalence of the illustrative configurations;
- evaluate the impact of an adopted regional policy;
- report confidential data from a separate research project;
- provide an automated recommendation engine for real organizations;
- establish causal effects for the illustrative pathways;
- replace legal review, local consent, or context-specific evidence.

## Methods and design principles

The academic model draws on:

- literature synthesis;
- legal and institutional analysis;
- problem decomposition;
- theory-of-change logic;
- backcasting;
- scenario and conditional-pathway design;
- adaptive decision logic;
- explicit treatment of uncertainty;
- sensitivity testing of normative assumptions.

The full academic model separates normative importance from context-specific priority. Its weighting and sensitivity exercises are design tools, not empirical estimates of causal effects.

See [`docs/methodology-note.md`](docs/methodology-note.md).

## Research foundations

Selected foundations include:

- Fung, A. (2006), “Varieties of Participation in Complex Governance.”
- Mansuri, G. & Rao, V. (2013), *Localizing Development: Does Participation Work?*
- Quist, J. & Vergragt, P. (2006), “Past and future of backcasting.”
- OECD (2022), *OECD Guidelines for Citizen Participation Processes*.
- OECD / European Union / EC-JRC (2008), *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators*.
- HM Treasury (2024), guidance on Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis.
- Federal Law No. 33-FZ of 20 March 2025, Article 50, as a legal foundation for TOS.
- Federal Law No. 172-FZ of 28 June 2014, Article 11, for the distinction between strategic planning instruments.

The live prototype contains links to the selected references.
